# VVTS airport and TMA integration

Run `main.py --mock --profile tsn_tma` and open `/` for TMA and `/airport` for the separate airport monitor. Both URLs use the same server and simulation clock, so they can be placed on different monitors. Scenario Builder remains at `/scenario`. The ☀ Day / ☾ Night button on either monitor stores one browser theme preference; both pages update when it changes. Aircraft tags show callsign, altitude, and ground speed on separate lines. Below FL100 altitude is shown in feet; at and above FL100 it is shown as a flight level. Click a symbol for the translucent flight detail panel.

The airport monitor uses AEON's ivory map theme, taxiway graph overlay, aircraft symbols and tags, flight strips, and highlighted routes. Scroll over the map to zoom, right-drag to pan, click an aircraft to select it, or drag its symbol onto a graph node to assign a new taxi route. Hold Shift while dragging to append a waypoint. A manually assigned route stops at its destination; **Restore planned route** resumes the normal departure or stand route. These changes are applied by the ground simulator and still obey its runway hold and clearance rules.

## Linked flow

- A departure is created at an AEON stand with callsign, aircraft type, destination, requested FL, departure route, SID, off-block time, and CTOT. It stays parked until off-block time, taxis on AEON's graph, and holds at HLD-S1 until cleared and its CTOT has passed. Its runway 25L roll accelerates toward 145 kt and ends at AEON's WP-2. The airport icon continues past the runway end through a nominal 36-second, 2,500 ft/min climb. The existing SID propagator takes over at 1,500 ft. The requested FL is FPL information; the SID's nominal altitude profile still drives TMA motion.
- The airport page can spawn an inbound aircraft with origin, requested FL, arrival route, and STAR. Existing inbound STAR/ILS traffic also appears on the airport monitor within 8 NM of runway 25R; if created elsewhere without FPL fields, the integration supplies only the known procedure and destination. The extension beyond the layout is schematic; the TMA procedure remains authoritative while airborne. At touchdown, the aircraft starts its AEON runway-exit and taxi route while a low-altitude runway-roll track remains on TMA. Its TMA position and speed follow the airport rollout until the 25R exit event removes it. The aircraft then continues to its stand on the airport monitor.
- Scenario Builder offers five linked VVTS studies: baseline, immediate departure surge, stand-metered departure surge, CTOT timed release, and mixed departures/ILS arrivals with 25L crossings. Old TMA-only presets are no longer offered on the VVTS scenario page. Saved scenarios include airport positions, clearances, timings, FPL data, and movement events. Reset clears both monitors.

## AEON rules and study assumptions

- The graph, stand coordinates, runway/taxiway paths, Dijkstra routing, aircraft spacing, HLD-S1 takeoff hold, HLD-P5 crossing hold, and runway occupation logic came from `aeon_sim/MISC/core` and `aeon_sim/vvts_layout.json`. Runway detection now covers every runway path node. An arrival whose route crosses 25L needs an explicit crossing clearance; a stand reachable without crossing 25L does not.
- The current study configuration retains AEON's runway pairing: 25L departure and 25R arrival. The TMA profile likewise uses those runways. Other runway directions need coordinated procedure and ground graph work.
- AEON's engine-off, TaxiBot, e-taxi, and fuel accounting features are present in the copied agent model but are not exposed as airport controls. Ground aircraft currently use its engine-on taxi behavior at approximately 15 kt. The linked runway model accelerates departures at 2.6 m/s² toward 145 kt and brakes arrivals from approximately 140 kt at 1.1 m/s² until taxi speed. The current ground speed is included in airport state and displayed on both monitors. The VVTS mock SID ground speed is 180 kt below 10,000 ft and 220 kt above that; STAR legs use 250 kt above 10,000 ft and 210 kt below, with ILS legs reducing from 180 to 140 kt. This is an indicative simulation profile, not an IAS model. EUROCONTROL's [A320 performance database](https://learningzone.eurocontrol.int/ilp/customs/ATCPFDB/default.aspx?ICAOFilter=a320) lists initial climb at 175 kt IAS and 2,500 ft/min, 290 kt IAS and 2,000 ft/min climbing toward FL150, and 450 kt TAS in cruise; these speed types cannot be substituted directly for ground speed.
- Takeoff and ILS are simplified kinematics, not aircraft performance models. The final approach extension is a display device, and stand allocation chooses the nearest available stand by layout coordinates. These are scenario assumptions to revisit with real operating data.
- Off-block and CTOT are absolute seconds from scenario start. CTOT is a nominal wheel-off target: the HLD-S1 release subtracts the predicted runway-roll time, then the event log records actual deviation. The window for an acceptable slot is not calibrated to operating rules yet. Study presets grant departure clearance in advance so runway occupation, spacing, off-block, and CTOT timing determine the outcome. Stand metering uses a nominal 60-second release interval, not a published runway capacity. In the mixed study, crossing clearance is given after a 30-second hold; manually spawned aircraft still require manual clearance.
- Each flight records off-block, runway entry, takeoff, TMA handoff, SID exit, approach visibility, touchdown, runway exit, crossing, and stand arrival when applicable. Run `python -m scripts.run_airport_studies` from the project root to replay all five studies and write `output/airport_study_results.json` with per-flight events and comparable taxi/hold and CTOT measures.
